Top picks

  • Haleakalā summit at sunrise 4.9 ★ (12,278) nps maui now my pick
    Sunrise from the ~10,000 ft summit; afterward, the Keoneheʻeheʻe (Sliding Sands) trail drops from the visitor center into the crater. The descent is easy; the climb back at altitude is the hard part (trail detail from own knowledge).
    Sunrise reservation Plan B: required to enter the Summit District 3:00–7:00 a.m.; recreation.gov only (no phone, no gate sales). Released 60 days ahead at 7:00 a.m. HST and sells out in ~15 min. For Dec 16–22 those dates (~Oct 17–23) have passed, so use the smaller batch released 2 days ahead at 7:00 a.m. HST: Dec 14 for Dec 16, through Dec 20 for Dec 22. $1.00/vehicle, non-refundable even for weather; one reservation per account per 3-day period. Book on recreation.gov.
    Entrance is separate: $30/private vehicle, valid 3 days (2026), and the same pass covers Kīpahulu. The park closed twice in the 2026 storms (Aug and Sept); check nps.gov/hale conditions before you go.
  • Road to Hāna → Kīpahulu: Pīpīwai Trail 4.8 ★ (490) nps maui now atlas obscura
    At the far end of the Hāna Highway, in Haleakalā's coastal Kīpahulu District: 2 mi each way (~4 mi round trip, moderately strenuous) through a bamboo forest to 400-ft Waimoku Falls. The trail starts at ʻOheʻo Gulch, where tiered pools drop into the ocean. NPS advises against swimming there (flash floods, rockfall), and the pools are often closed.
    Status Plan B: Kīpahulu reopened ~Oct 30, 2026 after flooding; the campground was still closed. No dated notice on the Hāna Highway itself was found after the late-October rain. Check before going: nps.gov/hale conditions.
  • Pacific Whale Foundation — Molokini snorkel and whale watch 4.7 ★ (3,068) maui now
    A research and education nonprofit that runs naturalist-led boats from Māʻalaea. It offers two formats: the Molokini & Turtle Arches snorkel, at a submerged crescent crater off Mākena, and a 2-hr whale watch. Plan B Humpback season officially opens Dec 15, but numbers are still low in mid-December and build through Jan–Mar. Take the snorkel trip as the main outing, and count any whales as a bonus.
    Check in at the PWF store, 300 Maalaea Rd; harbor parking is paid. Prices and operating days could not be confirmed, so check pacificwhale.org. Lahaina Harbor has run limited commercial tours since a phased reopening in Dec 2025. Free shore option: spotting scopes on the lawn at the humpback whale sanctuary visitor center 4.8 ★ (232) in Kīhei (my pick; 2026 hours not confirmed).

Where to stay

Six nights, Dec 16–22 Plan B. You need a car from any of these. Haleakalā sunrise and the Road to Hāna both start on the central and upcountry side of the island, so stay there or on the south shore, not in the west.

  • Paʻia. A small north-shore surf town you can walk end to end, at the start of the Hāna Highway and about 15 min from the airport. Day to day: Mana Foods, a large health-food grocer, is in town. Baldwin Beach is a long stretch of sand for walks and runs. The Haʻikū Islander bus (Route 35) runs to Kahului and the airport. For Hāna you are already at the start of the road, and Baldwin Ave climbs from town to Makawao and the Haleakalā road. Tradeoff: crowded in the daytime with Hāna traffic and day-trippers. In December, north swells make swimming at Baldwin and Hoʻokipa dangerous, so this is a base for walking the beach, not swimming at it.
  • Kīhei. A long strip along the south shore and the driest, sunniest base in winter, with the calmest water for swimming. Day to day: Foodland, Safeway and Times supermarkets, gyms, and the beach walks along Kamaʻole and Keawakapu. The Kīhei Islander bus (Route 10) is one of the island's busiest routes and has added evening trips. The Humpback Whale Sanctuary visitor center lawn is in town for watching whales from shore. Tradeoff: strip-mall sprawl along South Kīhei Rd, and bars cluster around south Kīhei near Kalama Park, so book away from that stretch. Of these three bases it is the farthest from Hāna and from the summit (my estimate), so those days start earliest from here.
  • Makawao (upcountry). A paniolo (cowboy) ranch town in upcountry farm country, which sits at about 2,000–4,000 ft, with the shortest drive to the Haleakalā summit for the 3 a.m. sunrise start. Day to day: a small town center, with Hui Noʻeau Visual Arts Center (lei and art workshops) just outside town. The Upcountry Islander bus (Route 40) runs to the airport, and the big Kahului stores are about 20 min down the hill (my estimate). Tradeoff: you drive almost everywhere outside the town center, nights are cool, and the nearest swimmable beach is a long drive away. Polipoli forest above Kula is closed as of Oct 30, 2026.

Skip West Maui (Kāʻanapali, Nāpili, Kapalua) on this itinerary. The resorts there are open, but Kahului is 45–60 min away along one road, the Honoapiʻilani Hwy, and the route passes Lahaina. Lahaina is still rebuilding after the August 2023 fire. Front Street reopened to vehicles on Aug 1, 2026, but the burn zone and the Banyan Tree remain off-limits. Each pre-dawn Haleakalā start or Hāna day from the west adds that drive both ways. In December, the north-facing parts of Nāpili and Kapalua are also the wettest and windiest on that coast.

Practical

Tap water
Potable from the county system island-wide; refill a bottle rather than buy. Ask locally before drinking tap water in rebuilt parts of Lahaina, where post-fire water advisories have applied (my pick, own knowledge).
Tipping
Expected. Sit-down restaurants 18–20%: the average full-service tip in Hawaii was 18.8% in Q2 2026 against 19.1% nationally (Toast data via Beat of Hawaii, 2026). About $1 a drink for beer or wine, 20% on cocktails, $2–5 for valet (Hawaii Guide, 2026). Servers get the full state minimum wage of $16/hr from 1 Jan 2026 (This Week Hawaii, 2026). Check the bill for an 18–20% auto-gratuity on large parties, and for added service charges, before you tip on top.
Cash & cards
Cards and tap-to-pay work almost everywhere. Carry small bills for honor-box fruit and banana-bread stands on the Road to Hāna, farmers-market stalls, some food trucks and cash fares on the bus (my pick).

Getting around

Transit
The county's Maui Bus runs buses only, roughly hourly, linking towns such as Kahului, Wailuku, Kīhei, Lahaina/Kāʻanapali, Paʻia and Makawao. It is a commuter network and does not reach Haleakalā summit, the Road to Hāna, trailheads or most beaches. The Haʻikū Islander (35) and Upcountry Islander (40) stop at the airport, but luggage is restricted. Pay in cash on board, with no change given (unconfirmed for 2026), or buy passes in the Token Transit app. Could not confirm whether bank cards can be tapped on board. Single $2.00 · day pass $4.00 (fares effective 1 Mar 2024, no 2026 change found). Most routes stop running in the evening, around 7:30 p.m. on the Kīhei Islander according to an undated route table. Could not confirm the 2026 last-bus times.
Rides
Uber and Lyft both operate. Lyft lets you schedule rides up to 90 days ahead. Use the airport (OGG) pickup zone outside baggage claim and follow the app for the exact spot. Uber's own averages are OGG–Kahului about $13 and OGG–Lahaina about $73 (Uber, 2026). Could not confirm OGG–Kīhei or a typical short in-town fare. Cars are plentiful around Kahului, Kīhei and Kāʻanapali. Upcountry, in Hāna and at Haleakalā they are scarce, and you can get stranded at a trailhead.
Verdict: rent a car at OGG. A $4 bus day pass beats a $73 Uber to Lahaina, but the bus stops in the evening and misses everything the trip is for. Haleakalā sunrise needs you at the gate by 3–7 a.m., which no bus or reliable ride covers. Even a few airport-scale rides cost more than several days of a rental. Maui has no walkable centre. Paʻia, Makawao and Wailuku's old town can each be walked end to end in about 10–15 minutes, but the towns themselves are 20–60 minutes apart by road.

History

  • Settlement and kingdom. Polynesian voyagers settled the islands; the date is contested, with AD 1000–1200 the best-supported range (Kirch's review of the archaeology). Kamehameha I, a chief from Hawaiʻi Island, united the islands as one kingdom by 1810, when Kauaʻi ceded.
  • Missionaries and sugar. American Protestant missionaries arrived in 1820, and sugar plantations followed in the 1820s–30s, importing Chinese, Japanese, Portuguese and Filipino labor. This is why the population and the food are as mixed as they are. In 1980 there were 14 sugar and 4 pineapple plantations on more than 300,000 acres; by 2017 both crops were under 5,000 acres.
  • Overthrow to statehood. On 17 January 1893 sugar and pineapple businessmen, backed by US troops, overthrew Queen Liliʻuokalani. The US annexed Hawaiʻi in 1898, made it a territory in 1900 and admitted it as the 50th state on 21 August 1959 (about 92–93% voted yes). Some Native Hawaiian groups contest the legality of the annexation.
  • Hawaiian Renaissance. From the 1970s, hula, the Hawaiian language and traditional voyaging were revived, and Kahoʻolawe, used as a bombing range, was returned to the state in 2003 after protests. The voyaging canoe Hōkūleʻa first sailed to Tahiti in 1976 (Hōkūleʻa date: own knowledge). Immersion preschools began in 1982.
  • Lahaina and now. The 8 August 2023 Maui wildfire killed 102 people (CBS reports 101), damaged or destroyed about 2,200 structures and displaced about 12,000. A $4.037B global settlement was announced in August 2024; payment status is not confirmed from a primary source. Construction began on the 60 Front Street homes on 3 October 2026, and a blessing marked the first commercial rebuild on Front Street on 13 October 2026. Tourism has not recovered to its pre-pandemic arrivals.

Culture

  • Kapu signs and heiau. A kapu sign means off-limits, and heiau (stone temple platforms) are living religious sites, not ruins. Where no boundary is marked, the outer edge of the structure is the limit; do not climb the stones, take anything, or stack rocks, since offerings (hoʻokupu) are left there. (DLNR Wahi Pana brochure)
  • Hawaiian on every sign. Place names are written with the ʻokina (ʻ) and kahakō (macron), and Hawaiian words are in daily English: mahalo, kōkua (help), and mauka/makai (toward the mountain/toward the sea) used as directions (own knowledge). The language was nearly lost; ʻAha Pūnana Leo immersion preschools began in 1982, and about 3,000 students were in 28 public immersion schools in 2023. Honolulu Magazine puts native-level speakers at roughly 10,000, under 1% of the state; other counts run higher depending on how speakers are defined. (National Geographic, Honolulu Magazine)
  • Pidgin. Hawaiʻi Creole English grew on the plantations from Hawaiian, English, Portuguese, Cantonese, Japanese and Filipino. You will hear it in shops and from drivers. The Census Bureau recognized it in 2015 but counted only about 1,600 speakers, while Ethnologue's estimate is about 600,000 first-language speakers (date not confirmed), so the numbers are unreliable. (NBC News)
