# Country dossier — Poland

## GDP
- Nominal GDP: $1.13 trillion (2026, IMF) — source: worldometers.info/gdp/poland-gdp
- GDP per capita (nominal): $31,336-31,340 (2026) — cross-checked worldometers + tradingeconomics
- GDP per capita (PPP): $59,790 (2026)
- GDP growth rate: 3.3% (2026)
- Sector composition: services 58.8%, industry 28.7%, agriculture 2.9% (year of this breakdown not explicitly stated in source, treat as ~2025/2026)

## Economy drivers
- Manufacturing and exports lead — automotive, machinery, electronics
- Poland is a major nearshoring destination for European manufacturers moving away from Chinese supply chains, especially automotive components supplying German automakers
- Q1 2026 growth aided by EU fund inflows, lower interest rates, increased defense spending
- Other core industries: food/beverage processing, shipbuilding, iron/steel, chemicals, glass, textiles

## Notable startups/tech
- Poland has 12 unicorns ($1B+ valuation) as of Aug 2026, combined value $41.5 billion; 2 valued at $10B+; Warsaw is the top hub with 6 of the 12 — source: dealroom.co/unicorns/poland
- Most valuable three: Allegro, InPost, CD Projekt RED (note: CD Projekt is publicly traded, doesn't strictly fit "unicorn" definition but is Poland's flagship tech/gaming export — maker of The Witcher, Cyberpunk 2077, founded 2002)
- 2026 YTD (through July): $420 million raised across 29 equity funding rounds in Poland
- Booksy (booking platform) — $200M+ raised total

## History in beats
- 14th century — mermaid motif already on Warsaw's coat of arms
- 1944 (Aug 1 - Oct 2) — Warsaw Uprising: ~50,000 Polish fighters (only ~5,000 armed) vs ~46,000 German troops; lasted 63 days; 150,000-250,000 Poles killed; surrender signed Oct 2, 1944
- 1944 (post-uprising) — Germans ordered remaining inhabitants to leave; more than 85% of the historic center systematically destroyed
- 1945 — census shows 145,000 people had already returned to the ruins
- 1945-1950s — five-year citizen-led reconstruction campaign rebuilds the Old Town's churches, palaces, market square from photographs and paintings (Canaletto's cityscapes reportedly used as reference — common knowledge, not confirmed via this search pass, label as own-knowledge if used)
- 1980s — Royal Castle reconstruction completed
- Communist era (1945-1989) — Poland under Soviet-aligned government
- 1989 — transition to democracy
- EU accession — Poland joined the EU (year not confirmed this pass — commonly known as 2004, label as own knowledge if used without a fresh source)
- UNESCO — Historic Centre of Warsaw is a World Heritage Site specifically because of the meticulous post-war reconstruction — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/30/

## Culture-section candidates
- Tipping: 10-15% at restaurants is the norm, appreciated but not obligatory (service staff earn a living wage) — 2026 source: hootling.com/tipping/poland
- Payment quirk: saying "dziękuję" (thank you) when handing over cash signals you don't want change — worth flagging as a practical/etiquette note
- Taxis: ~10% tip common
- Bars: round up to nearest 5-10 PLN
- Free walking tours: tip-based, 30-50 PLN/person expected
- Some upscale/tourist-area restaurants already include a service charge — no additional tip needed then

## Languages
- Official language: Polish
- English: strong among under-40s in Warsaw (students, professionals); high in tourist-facing businesses (hotels, restaurants, transit hubs) in central Warsaw; much weaker among older generations (many learned Russian as the mandated foreign language under communism, not English)
