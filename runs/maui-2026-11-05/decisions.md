# Maui — Where to stay — decisions (2026-11-05)

Sources: maui/dossier/city.md only (Neighborhoods, Getting around, Practical, status flags). Reddit stay and living-here threads were unavailable because reddit.com is blocked at the extension level, so the candidates rest on the dossier's own neighborhood notes, which are mostly tagged my pick, plus the Maui County bus route data.

## Shortlist (3 + 1 avoid)
1. **Paʻia**: walkable town, Mana Foods, Baldwin Beach for walks and runs, Route 35 bus to the airport, the start of the Hāna Hwy, and Baldwin Ave up to the Haleakalā road. Tradeoffs: daytime Hāna traffic, and December north swell makes swimming dangerous.
2. **Kīhei**: the dry, sunny base in winter with the calmest water. It has three supermarkets, gyms, beach walks, and the Route 10 bus with evening trips. Tradeoffs: sprawl, and bars cluster near Kalama Park (south Kīhei), so the entry says to book away from that stretch. It is the farthest of the three from the summit and Hāna; that is my estimate and is labeled.
3. **Makawao (upcountry)**: the closest base to the summit for sunrise. It has a small town center and Hui Noʻeau, and Route 40 goes to the airport. Tradeoffs: car-dependent, cool nights, and far from swimmable beaches. Polipoli is closed per DLNR notices (9/23, 10/5, 10/30/26). This is the weakest of the three on the walkable test; I kept it because the brief asks me to weigh sunrise access and the user plans upcountry drives.
4. **Avoid: West Maui (Kāʻanapali, Nāpili, Kapalua) for this itinerary.** It is a genuine booking trap: the most-marketed resort area, but 45–60 min each way on the one road through Lahaina for the sunrise and Hāna days. The Lahaina status (Front St reopened 8/1/26; burn zone and Banyan Tree off-limits) is stated factually, with no sightseeing framing. December weather is noted: the north-facing Nāpili and Kapalua areas are wetter and windier. The dossier's comparison of December west-side and Kīhei weather is limited to "south and west typically calmer in winter", plus Kīhei being dry and sunny and Nāpili/Kapalua wettest on its north-facing bits. I wrote nothing beyond that.

## Cut
- **Haʻikū**: quiet and green, but there is nothing walkable, it is wetter, and it is car-dependent. Paʻia gives the same north-shore and Hāna position with daily life on foot.
- **Wailuku/Kahului**: real daily life (Costco, Target, Down to Earth, the transit hub, Wailuku old town), but it is windy, has no beach, and lacks a vacation feel. Paʻia is 15 min away and covers the same central position better. ʻĪao is also of uncertain status (conflicting DLNR notice dated 11/2).
- **Wailea**: expensive, resort-only, and little ordinary daily life fails the week-of-living test. Kīhei next door has the same calm, dry water with groceries.
- **Kāʻanapali, Nāpili/Kapalua**: covered by the avoid line above, not listed as a pick.
- **Lahaina**: not a lodging candidate. It is a recovery zone.
- **Kula/Pukalani**: folded into the upcountry entry rather than listed separately. They are more spread out than Makawao.
