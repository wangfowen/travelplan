## Atlas Obscura
- Olowalu Petroglyphs (Puʻu Kīlea) — petroglyphs carved into a basalt cliff, West Maui south of Lahaina; easiest-to-reach petroglyph site on Maui — atlas obscura — https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/olowalu-petroglyphs
- Kahoʻolawe — island the US Navy used as a bombing target ~50 yrs; volunteers clearing ordnance and replanting; visible offshore from Kīhei/Wailea, not visitable except via Protect Kahoʻolawe ʻOhana volunteer trips — atlas obscura — https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/kaho-olawe
- Alexander & Baldwin Sugar Museum (Puʻunēnē, Kahului) — small museum beside the former sugar mill on the plantation era, labor camps, irrigation ditches — atlas obscura — https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/alexander-baldwin-sugar-museum
- Hāna Lava Tube (Kaʻeleku Cave) — walk-through lava tube on the Road to Hāna — atlas obscura — https://www.atlasobscura.com/things-to-do/hawaii/subterranean-sites
- Kanaio Coast — columnar basalt from a Haleakalā lava flow, remote south coast past La Perouse — atlas obscura — https://www.atlasobscura.com/things-to-do/hawaii (Maui listing; exact entry URL not confirmed)
- ʻOheʻo Gulch (Seven Sacred Pools) — tiered pools/waterfalls into the ocean, Kīpahulu district of Haleakalā NP; NPS: swimming not recommended (flash floods, rockfall), pools often closed — atlas obscura + nps — https://www.nps.gov/hale/planyourvisit/hiking.htm
- Charles Lindbergh grave (Palapala Hoʻomau Church, Kīpahulu) — aviator buried in a small coral-stone churchyard past Hāna — atlas obscura — https://www.atlasobscura.com/things-to-do/hawaii (exact entry URL not confirmed)
- Lahaina Banyan Tree — largest banyan on the island (fire-scorched 2023, regrowing); NOT open to visitors 2026, visible only from harbor tours per one guide — atlas obscura + hawaiisbesttravel — https://hawaiisbesttravel.com/visiting-lahaina-maui-2026/
- Kalaupapa (Molokaʻi, Maui County) — former Hansen's disease settlement, 8,000+ exiled; on Molokaʻi, not Maui — day-trip-only candidate, access status not checked — atlas obscura

## Things to do
### Haleakalā National Park
- Sunrise reservation — required for Summit District entry 3:00–7:00 a.m.; recreation.gov only (no phone, no gate sales); released 60 days ahead at 7:00 a.m. HST, sells out in ~15 min; a smaller batch released 2 days ahead at 7:00 a.m. HST; $1.00/vehicle non-refundable (incl. weather); one reservation per account per 3-day period. For 12/16–12/22: 60-day release dates fall ~10/17–10/23/2026 (already past) → rely on the 2-day release (e.g. 12/14 for 12/16). — nps — https://www.nps.gov/hale/planyourvisit/haleakala-sunrise-reservations-faq.htm ; https://www.recreation.gov/ticket/facility/253731
- Entrance fee 2026 — $30 private vehicle (3 days), $25 motorcycle, $15 per person on foot/bike, $55 park annual / Hawaiʻi Tri-Park annual; separate from $1 sunrise res; no nonresident surcharge listed for Haleakalā; fee-free days don't cover 3–7 a.m. — nps (page updated 12/18/2025) — https://www.nps.gov/hale/planyourvisit/fees.htm
- Summit hikes — Keoneheʻeheʻe (Sliding Sands) trail down into the crater from the visitor center (descent is easy, climb back at ~10,000 ft is the cost); Halemauʻu switchbacks to the crater rim from the 8,000 ft trailhead; Pā Kaʻoao short summit-area walk — my pick (trail names from own knowledge; NPS hiking page lists trails) — https://home.nps.gov/hale/planyourvisit/hiking.htm
- Summit Visitor Center reopened May 2026 — nps / maui now — https://mauinow.com/2026/05/20/haleakala-national-park-announces-the-reopening-of-the-summit-visitor-center/
- Kīpahulu District (end of Road to Hāna, same park pass) — Pīpīwai Trail through bamboo forest to 400-ft Waimoku Falls, 2 mi one way / ~4 mi RT, moderately strenuous; ʻOheʻo pools; reopened ~10/30/26 after flooding, campground closed then. NPS has run ti-leaf lei-making demos at Kīpahulu (hands-on cultural candidate, check schedule). — nps / maui now — https://www.nps.gov/hale/planyourvisit/hiking.htm ; https://www.nps.gov/hale/learn/news/ti-leaf-lei-making-featured-this-saturday-in-kipahulu.htm

## Neighborhoods (week-of-living lens) — all my pick unless tagged
- Paʻia — walkable surf town on the north shore, Hāna Hwy start; Mana Foods (big health-food grocer) in town; Baldwin Beach for walks/runs; on Haʻikū Islander bus (Route 35, airport 15 min). Tradeoff: Hāna-traffic and tourist bustle in daytime, winter north-shore surf makes swimming risky. Mana Foods/Baldwin = my pick.
- Haʻikū — rural, green, quiet north-shore/upcountry edge; vacation rentals among jungle; Haʻikū Islander bus reaches Pauwela Rd/Hāna Hwy. Tradeoff: car-dependent, wetter, nothing walkable.
- Kīhei — long south-shore strip, dry/sunny in winter, calmer water than north; supermarkets (Foodland, Safeway, Times), gyms, Keawakapu/Kamaʻole beach walks; Kīhei Islander (Route 10) is one of the two busiest routes, added evening trips. Tradeoff: strip-mall sprawl along South Kīhei Rd, some bar clusters (south Kīhei/Kalama area). — maui county route data — https://www.mauicounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/8641/Kihei_Islander-Route-Map-
- Wailea — manicured resort enclave south of Kīhei; Wailea Beach Path paved shoreline walk/run; calm winter water; Shops at Wailea. Tradeoff: expensive, resort-only, little ordinary daily life.
- Kahului/Wailuku — commercial/county seat; Wailuku old town has local eateries, near ʻĪao; Kahului has Costco, Target, Down to Earth, Maui Mall; bus hub (Kahului Transit Center). Tradeoff: not beachy, windy, not a vacation feel.
- Kāʻanapali — West Maui resort strip with beach walk path; open in 2026. Tradeoff: ~45–60 min to Kahului via one road (Honoapiʻilani Hwy), near Lahaina recovery zone, resort pricing; Uber OGG→Lahaina ~$73.
- Nāpili/Kapalua — quiet West Maui bays (Nāpili Bay, Kapalua Bay), condos, coastal trail; Kapalua resort open. Tradeoff: far from everything, wettest/windiest in winter on north-facing bits, few groceries (Nāpili Market/Times in Kahana).
- Upcountry (Makawao/Kula/Pukalani) — cool 2,000–4,000 ft, farm country, closest base for Haleakalā sunrise; Upcountry Islander bus (Route 40) via Makawao/Pukalani to airport. Tradeoff: car-dependent, chilly nights, far from swimmable beaches.

Busiest routes: West Maui Express and Kīhei Islander (600+ daily boardings each). ## Practical
- Winter ocean safety — Nov–Mar north swells hit north-facing shores (Hoʻokipa, Paʻia/Baldwin, Honolua/Kapalua side): big surf, rip currents, shorebreak; Hoʻokipa = expert surf/windsurf spot, very dangerous to swim in winter (lifeguard towers both ends; sheltered pocket at east end under the pavilion). South shore (Kīhei/Wailea/Mākena) and west typically calmer in winter. Check hioceansafety.com and NWS high-surf advisories. — maui now / gohawaii / hawaiianbeachrentals — https://mauinow.com/2013/12/20/mauis-north-facing-shores-can-expect-big-surf/ ; https://www.gohawaii.com/islands/maui/things-to-do/beaches/hookipa-beach-park-maui ; https://hioceansafety.com
- Mākena Big Beach (Oneloa) — notorious shorebreak injuries — my pick.
