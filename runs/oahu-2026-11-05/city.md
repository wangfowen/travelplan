## Things to do — hikes, permits, reservations
- Diamond Head State Monument — crater-rim summit trail, ~1.6 mi rt, WWII bunkers — hawaii state parks — https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/dsp/parks/oahu/diamond-head-state-monument/ — nonresidents need an advance online reservation (since 2022; walk-in/bus/rideshare arrivals book entry only, drivers book entry plus parking). Fee 2026: most secondary sources say $5/person and $10/vehicle; one says higher. Could not confirm on an official page (gostateparks.hawaii.gov rendered empty). Booking portal: https://gostateparks.hawaii.gov/diamondhead
- Hanauma Bay Nature Preserve — snorkel in a protected crater bay, mandatory education video — honolulu.gov DPR (page timed out); secondary: https://www.hawaii-guide.com/oahu/sights/hanauma-bay-reservations — closed Mon and Tue; Wed–Sun 6:45am–4pm; nonresident $25 (age 13+), kids free; parking $3 cash; reservations open 2 days ahead at 7am HST and sell out in minutes. The $25 fee is corroborated by KHON (https://digital-release.khon2.com/local-news/city-to-increase-non-resident-fee-to-hanauma-bay-to-25). Official confirmation failed. Winter swell rarely affects it (it faces south-east).
- Haʻikū Stairs (Stairway to Heaven) — CLOSED and illegal; the city began removing the 3,922-step stairs in 2024 (Nakoa Companies, $2.58M contract, Nov 2024); the Moanalua Valley section of the watershed is closed during removal; trespass arrests reported — star-advertiser — https://staradvertiser.com/?p=1272138 ; https://staradvertiser.com/2024/06/12/photo-gallery/access-to-haiku-stairs-region-in-moanalua-closed-for-removal — could not confirm whether removal is finished by 2026; treat as gone or closed either way.
- Lanikai Pillbox (Kaiwa Ridge) — 30–45 min steep climb to WWII pillboxes over the Mokulua islets; sunrise hike — honolulu magazine — https://www.honolulumagazine.com/?p=40906 — open in 2026 per blogs, with short pest-treatment closures; slick after rain. 2026 claim (blogs only, unconfirmed officially): parking banned on the Lanikai loop (Mokulua and Aʻalapapa Drives) from Jul 1, 2026, so plan to park in Kailua town and walk, or bike. https://wanderlustyle.com/lanikai-pillbox-hike-kaiwa-ridge/
- Mānoa Falls — ~1.6 mi rt rainforest trail to a 150-ft falls at the head of Mānoa Valley; muddy — honolulu magazine — https://www.honolulumagazine.com/?p=37561 — no permit; Paradise Park lot ~$7/vehicle (2026, secondary), lot ~8am–6pm; free street parking lower on Mānoa Rd adds ~1 mi each way. TheBus route 5 reaches the valley (my pick, verify). Swimming under the falls is prohibited (leptospirosis/rockfall).

## Ocean — winter conditions (Dec)
- North Shore winter surf season runs Oct–Mar; Waimea Bay, Pipeline/ʻEhukai, Sunset are big-wave venues, with "no swimming" and shorebreak/current warnings in winter; officials say do not swim or snorkel on the North Shore in winter and send people to Waikīkī. Sharks Cove and Three Tables (summer snorkel spots) have no permanent lifeguard and are dangerous in winter swell. NWS thresholds: advisory at 15 ft or more and warning at 25 ft or more on north shores. Swells arrive suddenly after flat lulls. — star-advertiser; hawaii news now — https://staradvertiser.com/?p=383338 ; https://hawaiinewsnow.com/2025/11/27/high-surf-advisory-up-north-west-shores-warning-level-swell-possible-this-weekend/
- Watching big surf: watch from a beach park with a lifeguard tower, e.g. Waimea Bay, ʻEhukai (Pipeline) — star-advertiser (as above).
- West side (Mākaha, Kaʻena) also takes winter NW swell — star-advertiser high-surf coverage.
- Winter-safe water: Waikīkī, Ala Moana Beach Park, Hanauma Bay (south/east-facing) — star-advertiser (as above). Kailua/Lanikai are trade-wind side and usually swimmable, but expect a Portuguese man-o'-war risk on onshore wind days (my pick, verify).
- Live conditions: NWS Honolulu surf forecast and hawaiibeachsafety.com (Honolulu Ocean Safety) — my pick.
- Humpback whale season starts ~Dec (my pick); see the events track for boats.

## Neighborhoods (for a week of daily life)

Star-Advertiser: https://www.staradvertiser.com/2026/06/24/hawaii-news/transit-fare-increase-takes-effect-july-1/
- HOLO card purchase — Skyline ticket vending machines (incl. Lelepaua airport station), and the HOLO app/retailers (my pick for the latter) — official airport page https://airports.hawaii.gov/hnl/the-bus-and-skyline/
- Skyline rail, 2026 — 13 stations Kualakaʻi (East Kapolei) → Kahauiki (Middle St/Kalihi Transit Center); Segment 2 opened Oct 16, 2025 adding Makalapa (Pearl Harbor), Lelepaua (airport), Ahua (Lagoon Dr), Kahauiki. Hours: daily 4am–10pm (every 10 min until 8:30pm, then every 15). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahauiki_station ; https://www.honolulu.gov/mayor/episode-129-skyline-segment-2-now-open-for-passenger-operations/ ; airports.hawaii.gov
- Airport → Waikīkī by TheBus W Line — runs airport → Downtown → Kakaʻako → Ala Moana → Waikīkī; stop on Ala Onaona St east of the lei stands; weekdays every 10–15 min (30 after 7pm), weekends every 15 (30 after 7pm); $3 HOLO / $3.25 cash; luggage limit one medium suitcase plus one carry-on that fit under or at the seat; Route 42 only one late-night Waikīkī trip. — https://airports.hawaii.gov/hnl/the-bus-and-skyline/ — the old Route 20 no longer listed as the airport–Waikīkī route.

## Practical
- Tipping (US norms, my pick — verify the year): restaurants 18–20% of pre-tax; bars $1–2/drink; rideshare 10–15%; hotel housekeeping $2–5/night; tours 15–20% to guide. Hawaiʻi GET tax ~4.712% Oahu is added at the register (my pick).
- Tap water — Honolulu Board of Water Supply water is potable (my pick).
- Cards — cards and Apple Pay accepted almost everywhere; cash for some food trucks, farmers markets, Hanauma parking ($3 cash), and the bus if no HOLO (my pick plus sources above).
- Safety basics — car break-ins at trailheads and beach lots; petty theft on Waikīkī beaches; visible homelessness around Chinatown/Kakaʻako/Ala Moana parks (my pick). Ocean safety above: swim at lifeguarded beaches, never turn your back on the ocean, obey "no swimming" flags; flash-flood risk on stream and valley trails (Mānoa) after heavy rain (my pick).
- Hiking-safety items: Koko Crater summit bunkers are taped off after the Jul 2025 injury (DPR); Lanikai Pillbox slippery when wet (blog).
