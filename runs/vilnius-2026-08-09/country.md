# Country dossier — Lithuania (research Aug 2026)

## GDP

- Nominal GDP ~€84.06B (2025 est.); GDP per capita ~€29,167 nominal (2025); growth ~2.92% (2025) — aggregated from IMF DataMapper and World Bank trackers, not a single primary Eurostat/Statistics Lithuania release — treat as approximate, flag year.

## Economy drivers

- Laser technology/photonics — genuine world leader in ultrafast (femtosecond/picosecond) laser systems, exports to 100+ countries, >90% export intensity, used in microsurgery/ophthalmology/semiconductors — 2025
- Fintech — EU's leading fintech hub, 282 companies by 2024 (from a few dozen), 8,000+ direct jobs, serves ~40M people EU-wide; e-payments €166B (60% growth 2022→Q3 2025) — driven by the Bank of Lithuania's fast licensing regime — 2025
- Biotech/life sciences — ~3.7% of GDP, ~90% export share, targeting 5% of GDP by 2030, 15,000+ specialists — 2025

## Notable startups

- Vinted (Vilnius-based secondhand marketplace, founded 2008, Lithuania's first unicorn 2019) — €8B valuation after an €880M secondary share sale, reported April 2026 (2025 financials: €1.1B revenue, €62M net profit); valuation path €3.5B (2021) → €5B (2024) → €8B (2025)
- Nord Security (Vilnius, maker of NordVPN) — $3B valuation since a Sept 2023 raise, holding at $3B with $357M ARR and ~1,800 employees as of 2026

## History in beats

1253 Mindaugas crowned King of Lithuania (traditional start of statehood) · late 1300s Grand Duchy of Lithuania becomes one of the largest states in Europe · 1569 Union of Lublin creates the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth · 1772-1795 partitions absorb Lithuania into the Russian Empire until 1918 · 16 Feb 1918 Act of Independence, modern Republic founded · 1940 Soviet occupation begins, 1941 Nazi German occupation · 1941-1944 Holocaust in Vilnius — pre-war Vilnius was the "Jerusalem of the North" (100+ synagogues, ~80,000 Jewish residents); mass killings at Ponary began weeks after the June 1941 occupation, the Vilna Ghetto was liquidated 23 Sept 1943, an estimated 600 of the ~80,000 survived · 1944 re-occupied by the USSR · 11 March 1990 first Soviet republic to declare restored independence · 13 Jan 1991 "January Events" — Soviet troops storm the Vilnius TV Tower and Parliament, 14 civilians killed · 29 March 2004 NATO membership · 1 May 2004 EU membership · 1 Jan 2015 euro adopted

## Culture-section candidates

- Basketball as "second religion" — men's team EuroBasket golds 1937/1939/2003; ~41% of the population watched the 2015 EuroBasket final, most-watched broadcast in Lithuania this century
- Republic of Užupis — see city.md; world's first constitution to mention AI, per Euronews
- Amber ("Lithuanian gold") — Lithuania a major source of Baltic amber, tied to the ancient Amber Road trade route, strong folklore (Jūratė the sea goddess) — Forbes, 2025
- Lithuanian Song and Dance Celebration — UNESCO-recognized (2003/2008), joint Baltic tradition of massed choral singing and folk dance held periodically in Vilnius
- Lithuanian language — widely described as one of the most archaic/conservative living Indo-European languages, retaining unusual closeness to reconstructed Proto-Indo-European forms; phrase carefully, "most archaic" not literally "oldest"

## Languages and English proficiency

- Lithuanian is the official/primary language.
- English proficiency skews heavily generational — roughly 80% of under-35s speak English (Soviet-era schooling didn't teach it, so it's rare among older generations); in Vilnius specifically (capital, young/urban, tourism and tech hub) English gets a traveler through restaurants, transit, and shops without much difficulty, especially in the center.
- Russian remains widely understood (~70% historically, Soviet-era schooling) but usage is politically loaded for some Lithuanians given the occupation history and the current Russia-Ukraine war — default to English first.
- Polish is spoken by the ethnic Polish minority in southeast Lithuania (~14% of citizens fluent).
